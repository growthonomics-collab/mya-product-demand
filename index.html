Demand Made Visible

Which pieces customers actually look for inside each collection, in any period since August 2023, ranked from GA4 and Search Console. Every product carries a Popularity Ratio for the chosen period (1.00 = an average product of its collection) that the Shopify store can sort, badge and link by.

Portfolio matrix

Where every piece sits, BCG style

Share = the Popularity Ratio in the product's main collection for the chosen period (1.00 = collection average). Growth = momentum, the ratio in this period divided by the ratio in the period of equal length just before it (1.00 = holding its position). Products that entered during the period are question marks by definition.

The formula
Demand Share % = 100 × [ 0.35 × Views ÷ ΣViews(collection) + 0.25 × Users ÷ ΣUsers(collection) + 0.15 × Engaged sessions ÷ ΣEngaged(collection) + 0.15 × Organic clicks ÷ ΣClicks(collection) + 0.10 × Add-to-cart ÷ ΣATC(collection) ], all summed over the chosen months
Popularity Ratio = Demand Share % × Products in collection ÷ 100
Products that entered during the period: signals scaled to the full period, then Ratio = (real views × own ratio + 100 × 1.50) ÷ (real views + 100)
Momentum = Ratio this period ÷ Ratio in the equal-length period before it

Each product is scored against the other products in the same collection in that period: the products the collection lists on the store today, plus every product that GA4 saw being opened from that collection page (URL /collections/…/products/…) during the period. That is how a collection can be ranked for a season whose products have since left the store. Ratio 1.00 = an average product of that collection; 3.00 and above = Hero, 1.50 to 2.99 = Strong, 0.50 to 1.49 = Average, under 0.50 = Low. Organic clicks exist from June 2025 (Search Console keeps 16 months); for earlier periods that weight is redistributed.

Reading a period
  1. Pick a season or a custom range. Every tab recomputes: rankings, tiers, matrix, collections, attributes. Nothing is pre-baked.
  2. Products no longer on the store are included and tagged "Not on site", so past seasons show what really sold attention then, not only what survived.
  3. Stock is today's stock. A sold-out tag means sold out on the crawl date, whatever period you look at.

Share of product page views in the chosen period by attribute. Product type is known for every product (from the catalogue, or from the URL for products no longer on the store). Colour, price, season tag and stock are known for live products only.

The formula

Every product is scored inside its collection for the chosen months, never against the whole store, so a strong skirt is not buried under dresses. The score is a Demand Share: the percentage of the collection's total demand in the period that one product captures. Shares inside a collection add up to 100.

Demand Share (product, collection, period) =
  0.35 × Views(product) / Σ Views(collection)
 + 0.25 × Users(product) / Σ Users(collection)
 + 0.15 × Engaged sessions(product) / Σ Engaged sessions(collection)
 + 0.15 × Organic clicks(product) / Σ Organic clicks(collection)
 + 0.10 × Add-to-cart(product) / Σ Add-to-cart(collection)
 × 100, every term summed over the months in the period
SignalSourceWeightWhy
Page viewsGA4 · Pages and screens, by month35%Raw interest. Counts repeat looks, which is how people shop for occasion wear.
Active usersGA4 · Pages and screens, by month25%Breadth of interest. Monthly users are summed over the period, so a user active in two months counts twice, equally for every product.
Engaged sessionsGA4 · Pages and screens, by month15%Quality of interest. A product people stay on, scroll and click earns more than one they bounce from.
Organic clicksSearch Console (GA4 link) · Landing pages, by month15%Demand from Google. Available from June 2025 (Search Console keeps 16 months). In earlier periods, or in collections with no product-level clicks, the weight is redistributed.
Add-to-cartGA4 · Ecommerce items, by month10%Purchase intent. Low weight because sold-out products cannot be added to cart.

If a collection has no data for a signal in the period, that weight is dropped and the others are rescaled, so the shares still add up to 100.

Who is in a collection, for a past period

Shopify only knows today's membership. For any period the collection's members are the products it lists today plus every product that GA4 saw opened from that collection page during the period (the store's URLs carry the collection: /collections/ss26-sirens-dresses/products/elodie-dress). A product that has since been unpublished still appears in the seasons it was sold, tagged "Not on site". The same rule builds the product-type rankings (all dresses, all tops), where the type of an old product is read from its URL.

Products that entered during the period

A product first seen in the middle of the period has fewer months to collect views than its neighbours. Its signals are scaled to the full period (× months in period ÷ months since first seen), then blended with a prior of 1.50 in proportion to its real views: (views × own ratio + 100 × 1.50) ÷ (views + 100). With under 100 real views it stays at 1.50 and is labelled New. This replaces the launch-date rule of the first version and works for any period.

Momentum, and the seasonal trap

Momentum compares the product's ratio in the chosen period with its ratio in the period of equal length immediately before it (for Mar to Aug 2026 that is Sep 2025 to Feb 2026). Rising is at least 25% up, Fading at least 20% down, Steady is between, New means it had no ratio before. Because the comparison period is usually the opposite season, a summer dress fading against a winter period is normal; use the Seasons tab and compare like with like (SS26 against SS25) before drawing conclusions.

Sources and coverage

    Refreshing the data

    1. GA4 → Reports → Engagement → Pages and screens. Add the metrics Sessions, Engaged sessions, Add to carts, Ecommerce purchases; secondary dimension Month; filter Page path contains "/products/". Export one CSV per calendar year (the export is capped at 100,000 rows). Repeat with the filter "/collections/".
    2. GA4 → Monetization → Ecommerce purchases (Item name), secondary dimension Month, one CSV per year.
    3. GA4 → Search Console → Google organic search traffic (Landing page), one CSV per month (this report does not accept the Month dimension).
    4. Drop the files in build/hist, run build/crawl.py (catalogue and stock), build/build_history.py and build/build_page.py. The Shopify sort package (build/build_shopify.py) still runs on the 90-day view, which is the one that should drive the store's order.

    Using it on the store

    1. Collection ordering: use the last 90 days (the default period) and the shopify/ package in the repo. Seasons are for planning, the current period is for sorting.
    2. Buying and re-cuts: open the season you are planning against (SS26 for next summer), filter Products by "Not on site" to see what sold attention and is gone, and by "Sold out" for what is still listed but cannot sell.
    3. Merchandising gaps: in Collections, a collection page with strong views whose products carry low Demand Share is sending traffic to pieces people do not want to look at. Refresh the selection there first.
